The observation is `labelled-topk-v2`: one uint8 tensor of canonical JSON (about 84.1 kB: 512 sampled token ids and 2560 labelled top-5 logprobs as float64 hex). A comparison therefore reports either identical, N bytes differing at equal length, or a length change. With the atomic path live, every comparison differs, most by length (84102 to 84110 bytes) and some by tens of thousands of bytes. With it off, the differing comparisons are same-length differences of 701, 719 or 738 bytes and, in the det arm, one 3-byte length change in each process. These byte counts are not token counts: a token id changing to another id with the same digit count would also give a same-length difference, so the reports cannot show that the residuals are value-only.

## The wheel edit

`wheel_guard.patch`: `import os` added after `import logging`, and line 475 of the installed `sglang/srt/layers/quantization/marlin_utils.py`, `if not True:`, replaced by `if not os.environ.get("SGLANG_MARLIN_USE_ATOMIC_ADD", "0") == "1":`. A full diff of the saved stock and edited copies shows exactly these two hunks and nothing else. `sha256sums.txt` holds the two digests (stock `1f0529ca...`, 32716 bytes; edited `c3386c20...`, 32780 bytes), matching `ablation.log`. The compiled `marlin_utils.cpython-312.pyc` was removed at 20:09:02Z and regenerated; the diagnostic read its header (mtime 1789416542, that is 20:09:02Z, source size 32780) at its start and end. Guard tests in `ablation.log`, calling `should_use_atomic_add_reduce(16, 1536, 8960, cuda, fp16)` (the down_proj shape at TP=1; `m` is not used by the guard): stock and variable unset, True; edited and unset, False; edited and `1`, True; edited and `0`, False. `maybe_warn_marlin_atomic_add_env` was left as installed; it is called only in the off branch and returns at once, so it cannot alter the return value. The edited guard accepts only the string `1`.

## PATCH MISMATCH lines

`diag.log` (20:53:29Z) and the failed first diagnostic attempt both print `PATCH MISMATCH` after re-applying the edit. The check pipes `diff -u stock F` into a textual diff against `wheel_guard.patch`; the `+++` header of the patch carries the file mtime with nanoseconds (`20:09:02.829711907`), and the copy round-trip through the pod's network volume, whose stored mtimes are whole seconds, drops the fraction, so the header line differs while the content does not. The sha256 of the installed file immediately after each re-apply is `c3386c20...`, identical to the file used in arms 2 to 4, and the final pyc check matches. The diagnostic's guard-off arms therefore ran the same edited source as the census arms. The census arms finished at 20:13:32Z and the first diagnostic attempt began restoring the stock file at 20:13:36Z, so they never overlapped.

## What the ablation establishes and what remains unexplained

On this stack only (H100, driver 580.126.09, torch 2.13.0+cu130, sglang 0.5.19 with sglang-kernel 0.4.6.post1, model revision 4f5a0e31, TP=1, 16 prompts, 32 greedy tokens, radix cache off): with only the guard line changed, the two processes of each guard-off arm start from the same baseline hash, differing comparisons fall from 12 of 12 to 4 of 12 (stock scheduler) and 6 of 12 (deterministic mode), distinct outputs over 14 evaluations fall from 14 to 3, and the diagnostic's sampled-token changes fall from 30 tokens in 3 of 6 repeats to 0 in 6 of 6. Setting the variable to 1 on the same edited wheel restores 12 of 12 differing comparisons, 14 distinct outputs, different baseline hashes across processes and 30-token changes in 2 of 6 diagnostic repeats; the stock file restored for the diagnostic again gives 30-token changes in 3 of 6. The Marlin atomicAdd K-slice reduction, as switched by `should_use_atomic_add_reduce`, is therefore the isolated cause of the recorded token-level differences and of the cross-process baseline divergence for this model on this stack.

Not established: that the atomic path is the only source of run-to-run difference. The same-length differences that remain with the path off (4 of 12 and 6 of 12 comparisons) are not explained by the guard and are not attributed here; no scheduler instrumentation or one-sequence-per-batch arm was run for SGLang in this ablation, so nothing in these records identifies their cause. A later arm on another pod ran the guard-off configuration one request at a time (`sglang_qwen2.5_1.5b_gptq_marlin_guard_off_fixed_composition_x6`, [campaign note](../shape/campaigns/2026-09-15-h100-e6-matrix2.md)) and gave one observation in all 14 evaluations; that fits a batching effect but does not isolate batch composition. The two census guard_off_det processes differed in 2 of 6 and 4 of 6 comparisons while the single diagnostic process under the same flag was identical in 6 of 6 repeats; candidates are process-to-process variation of a scheduling effect (two samples against one) or `log_level=info` timing, and neither is shown. Not run: TP=2 and TP=4 down_proj shards (k=4480 and 2240) and the qkv shard of the 7B model at TP=4.
