## The per-tensor arm

**The first attempt did not force the scheme.** `run_e6.py` at 50aae53 called `shape_hook.register()` in `record()` and `replay()` before `_engine()` set `SHAPE_FORCE_FP8_PER_TENSOR`; the hook reads the variable once, in its first `register()`, and at TP=1 with the in-process engine core the plugin load at engine start returns without reading it again. The attempt therefore ran vLLM's per-token default: its hook logs equal the per-token arm's in `h`, `argmax` and `logits_h` for all 512 rows of record and replay, and its 16 continuations are the same (engine logs `F4c.*`). Its raw files are in `2026-09-15-h100-e6-matrix2/failed_pertensor_not_forced/`, without a summary, so that reanalysis does not list a second per-tensor row; `run_e6.py --compare` on them gives IDENTICAL, a second per-token measurement. The correction, two lines setting the variable before `register()` in `record()` and `replay()`, ran on the pod for the second attempt's record and replay (`f4c_fix.sh`); its `--compare` ran with the uncorrected tree. The E4 driver sets the variable before `register()` and the E2 driver before the engine starts, so neither was affected; the E3 driver accepted the flag, named the arm `_pertensor` and never set the variable, and no E3 per-tensor arm exists in the repository. The current tree sets it before `register()` in E3 and E6, and `tests/test_fp8_forcing_order.py` fails on the old order.

**Why the second attempt is taken as per-tensor.** No recorded field attests the scheme: the hook records no FP8 scale under compilation (`fp8_scale_first_call` is null in every pass, as in the compiled E4 per-tensor arms), writes no line when it forces, and neither `env.json` nor the hook's environment record keeps `SHAPE_FORCE_FP8_PER_TENSOR`. Per-tensor operation is therefore an inference from four observations. (1) Both `env.json` files record the corrected driver, by the digests above. (2) Pass 0 has the same inputs as in the per-token arm (the same prompts and schedule, 481 tokens), and all 8 rows differ in `h` and `logits_h`; over the trace `h` differs in 512 of 512 rows and the argmax is equal in 417. (3) Slots k and k+8 share a prompt; slot k is prefilled in pass 0 (481 tokens with its first-wave neighbours), slot k+8 in pass 5 (489 tokens with different neighbours). Their prefill rows have equal hidden-state hashes for all 8 pairs in the per-token arm, in the failed attempt, in the bf16 TP=2 arm and in the bf16 TP=1 record in `results/e6`, and for none of the 8 pairs in the per-tensor record or its replay (`twin_prefill_rows.py`). A pass-wide activation scale is the expected cause; a kernel choice that depends on batch size could also break equality, which is why this is a signature and not a proof. (4) The compile timing indicates a new graph: 8.82 s for the per-tensor record, against 0.82 s for the failed attempt straight after the per-token arm, consistent with reuse of that arm's Inductor artefacts; the CUDA graph pool was 0.61 GiB against 0.55 GiB. In the census's pinned vLLM source the online FP8 linear method chooses per-token scales when `cutlass_fp8_supported()` is true and per-tensor scales otherwise, and the CUTLASS FP8 kernel accepts either, which fits `Selected CutlassFP8ScaledMMLinearKernel for Fp8PerTensorOnlineLinearMethod` in all six FP8 logs; the 0.28.0 wheel's source was not inspected.

**Result.** With per-tensor dynamic activation scales in effect, by that inference, compiled with FULL_AND_PIECEWISE graphs, the replay that re-supplies every neighbour's recorded tokens reproduced all 37 passes bit for bit. Of its 16 continuations, 11 equal the per-token arm's. The replay equals its record in every row, which a per-token replay could not do, since per-token hashes differ from this record in every row. This bears on X2 without testing it: X2 predicts that a row's bits depend on its neighbours' content through the one scale, and E6 re-supplies that content.

The default NCCL configuration did not initialise on this pod and was not tested. These TP=2 records are not compared with the TP=1 records of the same model.

## What the replays were

New processes on the same pod, each started after its record had exited, with its own engine start-up and memory profile; the KV pool differed from the record's in five of six arms (for example 2,334,256 against 2,352,432 tokens at TP=2 plain), and the pool size is not a comparison field. Caches on disk were warm: each compiled replay compiled its graph in 0.30 to 0.83 s against 3.52 to 12.01 s for its record, and the FP8 checkpoint's DeepGEMM warm-up ran at 3.13 kernels per second in the record and 8,113.51 in the replay (both logs warn that DeepGEMM wants NVCC 12.9 or later). `VLLM_DISABLE_COMPILE_CACHE=1` was in effect and no log contains `Directly load AOT compilation`, but that variable covers vLLM's own compiled-artefact cache, not Inductor's or DeepGEMM's. The replays therefore show that a new process on the same pod reproduced the recorded passes; they do not show that a cold recompilation, another machine of the same SKU or another schedule would.

## Output check

Every continuation has 32 tokens and replay tokens equal record tokens in every arm. No continuation ends in a cycle of period 8 or less. Every repetition of period at most 16 over at least twice its period (104 continuations across the six arms' records and replays and the failed attempt's record; `continuation_cycles.py`) is the 15-token sentence of the prompts that repeat "The verifier records the batch shape at every step and replays it later."; in the TP=2 plain arm one other continuation repeats a 16-token phrase over its last 23 tokens. The fewest distinct tokens in any continuation is 13. The uncompiled MoE arm with graphs is not degenerate. In the chunked-prefill arms the second-wave prompts are all distinct.

- Result: both reports `bitwise-identical`, 6 of 6 comparisons identical, the same baseline hash `94ecbdb6...` in both processes. The batched guard-off arm (`d9194bde...`, `3bc69bfa...`) had 4 of 12 comparisons differing and 3 distinct observations (`c61a5a0c...`, `f11a892f...`, `a68138a1...`); `94ecbdb6...` equals none of them. The two arms' reports have the same 204 installed distributions and the same `ServerArgs` except `max_running_requests` (1 against None); the probe source differs (50aae53 against 6478153).
- What the setting changes: it caps the running batch at one request, and both engine logs show decode CUDA graphs captured for batch size 1 only. The reports do not record per-pass batch sizes, so one request per pass is the configured behaviour, not an observed one. One request at a time fixes batch composition, but batch membership and batch size change together here: every pass's row count changes, decode graphs exist only at batch size 1, and any kernel choice that depends on the row count, Marlin's included, may change with them. The result therefore fits a batching effect as the source of the guard-off arm's remaining differences and does not isolate composition. It is 12 comparisons against 12 on another pod, and the comparisons are not independent.
- Kernels: `sglang_jit_kernels.txt` names six JIT-compiled SGLang kernels present after the runs, among them `sgl_kernel_jit_gptq_marlin_fp16_t`; process a spent 4 min 5 s on its first graph-capture step (`sgl_arm_a.full.log`) and process b ran in 38 s, so b used the kernels a built. How the ablation's kernels were built is not recorded. The reports hold hashes only, so no text check was possible.
- A first SGLang start was stopped during its guard tests to run the two online FP8 arms first, and a second was stopped at 02:58Z during kernel compilation to run the corrected per-tensor arm; the second left an empty invocation directory (`3e5fdd20-f66d-4763-8b10-adb208c6ed00`), not committed.

## Not run

SGLang E6 (the SGLang arm is a probe-engine repeatability arm, not a record and replay); TP above 2, pipeline or expert parallelism; TP=2 with the default NCCL configuration (NVLS on); the same software on other GPU models; a replay of these records on another machine of the same SKU from cold caches, or on this pod after clearing the Inductor and DeepGEMM caches; more than one replay per record; other schedules; a boundary rebuild for these arms; compiled MoE; and any commitment to the KV cache, which E6 does not hash. The forced per-tensor result is for one model at TP=1 with one recorded schedule.

## Deviations

1. The first pod's driver was too old for torch 2.13.0+cu130; see "Pod and stack".
2. The first TP=2 attempt failed at NCCL initialisation; see "TP=2". Its arms left empty directories, not committed.
3. The TP=2 arms ran with `NCCL_NVLS_ENABLE=0`, which their `env.json` files do not record; see "TP=2".
4. A retry queue found those empty directories, and its fallback to the newest arm directory pointed at the MoE arm: both of its replays were refused before writing, and `--compare` rewrote the MoE arm's `summary.json` at 02:43:06Z. The audit found that the raw record and replay files predate the misfire and that the replay's record digests still match; the summary is reproduced byte for byte offline from those files. The fallback was removed (`matrix.sh` as committed) and the TP=2 arms rerun as T1b and T2b.
5. For about three seconds (02:39:30Z to 02:39:33Z) the NCCL test held a CUDA context on GPU 0 while the MoE record was between weight loading and memory profiling; record and replay sized the KV cache identically (232,816 tokens).
6. The first per-tensor attempt ran per-token; see "The per-tensor arm".
