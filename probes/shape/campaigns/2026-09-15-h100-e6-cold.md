# H100 campaign, 15 September 2026: E6 cold replays on a second machine

Every committed H100 E6 record replayed once more, on a different two-GPU H100 pod, with every compile and kernel cache emptied and verified empty before each replay, and with the same driver, the same vLLM 0.28.0 stack and the same installed packages as the records. This is the cold-cache, second-machine replay that the three earlier E6 campaigns list as not run. Every record and replay here ran vLLM 0.28.0, so nothing in this note is evidence about any other version. The campaign was audited independently before attachment and the audit's corrections are carried here. Every number here comes from the arm records, the files in [2026-09-15-h100-e6-cold/](2026-09-15-h100-e6-cold/), RunPod's billing records or the audit.

**Result. Six of the eleven records replayed bit for bit and five did not. The five are every compiled bf16 arm; the six are the four compiled FP8 arms and the two uncompiled MoE arms.**

## Cold-cache proof

Before each replay the queue deleted the vLLM, Inductor, triton, NVIDIA, DeepGEMM, TileLang and CUTLASS cache directories and everything one level under the two temporary directories, listed every location, and refused to launch the arm unless the total was zero files. All fourteen `cache_before` files end in `CACHE_TOTAL_FILES 0`. After each replay it listed every regular file written since a marker, excluding only the weights, the code, the results, the logs and the package cache.

- Each replay wrote 165 to 1,027 cache files. Aggregated over all fourteen, the only directories written are the Inductor and triton caches, the vLLM and FlashInfer caches, a lock file and a usage-stats file; nothing that can carry compiled state was written outside the cleared set.
- The NVIDIA compute cache was never written, and no log contains `Directly load AOT compilation`.
- FlashInfer's sampling JIT was rebuilt in every replay, and DeepGEMM rebuilt all 1,735 kernels for Qwen3-8B-FP8 plain at 11.61 kernels per second, the rate that arm's record reports (11.83) and not the 3,073.54 of its warm same-pod replay.
- Engine start-up took 91.56 to 101.63 s in the TP=1 Qwen2.5 and Llama arms and 284.35 s for Qwen3-8B-FP8 plain.
- Three of the fourteen `cache_before` files list twelve locations rather than fourteen (`q25_plain`, `q25_mixed`, `q25_fp8_pertensor`): a lock file and a usage-stats directory were added to the cleared set at 13:59Z after the first replay showed them written. Neither is a compiled artefact. `q25_plain` ran before either existed; for the other two they existed, unchecked and uncleared.

The split follows the compute path and not the record pod: records from each of the three record pods appear on both sides.

**The DIFFERS arms.** In each, `h` and `logits_h` differ from pass 0, slot 0, a prefill row, and the rows that remain equal are concentrated in one or two request slots. Teacher forcing held every token, so `outputs_identical` is false only because the recorded top-5 logprobs differ. The free-running argmax changed in at most one row per arm, and each change is a tie: in the TP=1 plain arm at pass 3, slot 0, the record holds 1083, 1172 and 5157 at the same logprob and takes 1083, while the cold replay separates them by 0.125 and takes 1172; the TP=1 chunked-prefill arm, the TP=2 plain arm at pass 19 and the TP=2 chunked-prefill arm at pass 4 are the same pattern, with the tie on one side and a single bf16 step of 0.125 on the other. The Llama arm has no argmax change.

**The compile-cache key.** `VLLM_DISABLE_COMPILE_CACHE=1` enters vLLM's compile-cache key. Nine of the eleven replays therefore used the same key as their record, because those records also set the variable; the two exceptions are the 14 September TP=1 plain and chunked-prefill arms, whose records did not. The first diagnostic below removes that difference for both.

## Diagnostics

Three further cold replays under the same checks (`queue_extra.log`, `verdicts_extra.txt`).

| Replay | Environment | Against the record | Against the other cold replays |
|---|---|---|---|
| plain, `replay_cold_vcache` | the record's own: `VLLM_DISABLE_COMPILE_CACHE` unset | DIFFERS, 497 of 512 rows | equal to `replay_cold2` in all 512 rows |
| plain, `replay_cold2` | as the main replay | DIFFERS, 497 of 512 rows | differs from `replay_cold` in 5 of 512 rows |
| chunked prefill, `replay_cold_vcache` | the record's own | DIFFERS, 555 of 555 rows | not paired |

1. **The environment variable is not the cause.** With it unset, the replay's whole resolved configuration is byte-identical to the record's, including the compile-cache key, and the verdict and the differing-row count are unchanged in both arms. The fresh compile wrote the same vLLM ahead-of-time key that the record pod's warm replay recorded in its `run.json`, for the plain arm and for the chunked-prefill arm. The configuration matched; only the compiled artefact could differ.
2. **Two cold replays of one record, on one machine, were not bit-identical to each other.** `replay_cold` differs from `replay_cold2` in 5 of 512 rows, at passes 14, 17, 24, 27 and 29, all decode rows in FULL-graph passes of 16 rows, in `h` and `logits_h` with no argmax change, while `replay_cold_vcache` equals `replay_cold2` in all 512 rows. Bit-identity in these campaigns is therefore an observation about the runs made, not a demonstrated property of the configuration, and this difference cannot be attributed to any difference between machines.
3. **The two archived cold compiles agree.** Their Inductor caches hold the same ten kernels under the same graph hashes, and every tuning decision is equal: block sizes, warps, stages, the configuration hash and the triton cache hash. Only the measured benchmark time differs ([inductor_best_configs/](2026-09-15-h100-e6-cold/inductor_best_configs/)). Three of the ten are fused add-and-RMS-norm reductions whose block size Inductor picks by benchmarking.

## What the campaign does and does not show

Established:

- Five compiled bf16 arms that replayed bit for bit in a warm process on their own pod did not replay bit for bit cold. For the two Qwen2.5-7B TP=1 arms that is cold and on a different machine; for Llama a different host is likely; for the two TP=2 arms it is cold on different GPUs.
- Four compiled FP8 arms and two uncompiled arms did replay bit for bit cold.
- The difference is not the environment variable, not the package set, not the code and not the weights.
- Two cold replays of the same record on the same machine can differ in a few rows.

Not established:

- Why the split falls where it does. Two accounts fit every arm. A benchmark-chosen reduction configuration could differ between the record's compile and a fresh one, which is untestable here because the record-side artefacts were not kept and the two compiles that were kept are the two runs that agreed. Alternatively the FP8 arms quantise activations to a coarse grid, which absorbs a small upstream difference that a bf16 path carries. The records do not separate them. The fused normalisation-and-quantisation pass does not explain the split: the recorded `pass_config` has `fuse_norm_quant` false in both online FP8 arms, which were IDENTICAL, and true in the two uncompiled MoE arms, which were never compiled.
- That the TP=2 records are on a different physical machine.
- A rate for anything here: one cold replay per arm, two for one arm and one more for another.

## TP=2: the ranks did not agree in one cold replay

In the TP=2 chunked-prefill cold replay, rank 1's hidden-state hash differs from rank 0's in 4 of 555 rows (passes 22, 33, 46 and 47), all decode rows, with `logits_h` and the argmax equal across ranks in all four. The record, its warm replay and the TP=2 plain arm on both sides have no such row. The comparison reads rank 0, so the verdict is unaffected and the arm differs from its record in all 555 rows in any case, but the consequence for the method is general: at TP above 1 an IDENTICAL verdict is a statement about rank 0. The earlier claim that a rank 1 hook log equals rank 0's in every field but the rank number and wall time holds for the records and the warm replays, and not here.

## Deviations

1. The first pod went to CA-MTL-1, outside both record datacentres, and had the wrong driver. The one retry went to AP-IN-1 for the best driver odds, which is why the replay pod may share a host with `fgia5nk90f4ryc`.
2. The second pod's terminate-after was 58 minutes rather than 60, to keep both pods under 7 USD.
3. The first prefetch launch hung and was relaunched 2.5 minutes later; nothing was written in between.
4. Machine identity, cache locations and a distribution diff were added to the post-bootstrap script.
5. Two pod-side fixes during the queue: at 13:56Z the login message module was disabled in the SSH daemon, because logins were writing a file into the cache tree, and at 13:59Z a lock file and a usage-stats directory were added to the cleared set, as described under the cold-cache proof.
6. Three diagnostic replays were added after the eleven arms.
7. A 720 s timeout wrapped each replay and never fired.
