# fp-atomics-census

A source census of floating-point atomic operations in LLM inference engines, with runtime observations on selected GPU stacks. It identifies potential obstacles to bit-exact replay; it does not certify a deployment or establish a complete verification protocol.

Eight repositories are pinned in [scan-manifest.json](scan-manifest.json): vLLM, SGLang, FlashInfer, FlashAttention, Marlin, DeepGEMM, DeepEP and selected CUTLASS reduction paths. The pins are being moved one repository at a time, each once every cited site that changed has been re-read (`make repin-plan` carries the unchanged sites and lists the rest): vLLM, SGLang and FlashInfer to the commits their release tags v0.29.0, v0.5.19 and v0.6.18.post1 point to, the releases planned for the runtime stack, and the others to the last commit on the default branch before 15 September 2026 00:00 UTC; each moved pin records its rule as `pin_rule`. CUTLASS has moved, to its commit of 8 September 2026, vLLM to the commit its release tag v0.29.0 points to, of 8 September 2026, FlashInfer to the commit its release tag v0.6.18.post1 points to, of 4 September 2026, which is the release the SM120 fused MoE measurements were made against, and FlashAttention, DeepGEMM and DeepEP to their last commits before the cut-off, of 14 September, 14 September and 4 August 2026; Marlin, which has had no commit since its September 2024 pin, keeps it; SGLang is still at its early July 2026 commit. The [inventory](inventory/) contains source classifications and supporting references. Scanner matches and unresolved triage are recorded separately. The GPU measurements were made on 6, 7, 14 and 15 September 2026 against installed wheels and, for one FlashInfer run, a source build of its main branch; their versions differ from the source census. The 14 and 15 September campaigns are described in [probes/shape/campaigns](probes/shape/campaigns/).

## What this adds

[Cankaya (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00279v1) already distinguishes genuine run-to-run nondeterminism from changes caused by batch size, and lists per-pass batch information for replay (§§3.2 and 6). [Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/) also treats batch invariance explicitly. This repository extends the source inventory and tests selected configurations. It does not present batch dependence as a new discovery or refute those works with experiments outside their stated scope.

The source reading identifies order-dependent accumulation in some adapter, quantisation, MoE, sampling and model-specific paths. Other paths use serialisation, one writer per destination, exact integer operations or opaque libraries. No order-dependent atomic was identified in the reviewed ordinary dense vLLM forward source paths under the stated configurations. That is a bounded source observation: generated kernels, library internals, unresolved candidates and other sources of nondeterminism prevent a universal conclusion. Throughput costs and protocol soundness were not measured here.

Unresolved candidates may be duplicate matches, helper definitions or additional sites. Their number is not a count of missed floating-point atomics. The ledger makes the unfinished triage explicit; source coverage is not claimed to be exhaustive.

Classes are **A** (potentially order-dependent floating-point accumulation), **A1** (one writer per destination), **A2** (serialised order), **A3** (gated path, including a gate that is designed but asserted off at the pin), **B** (exact or order-independent operation under the row's assumptions), **B-indirect** (an integer atomic can determine an order consumed by floating-point code), and **C** (opaque library or binary). Read `path`, `default_path_condition`, `gate`, `confidence` and `evidence` together. A default path is reachable on the named profile, not on every deployment. A source classification is not a measured runtime verdict.

`make validate` checks the schema, pinned checkout HEADs, exact ordered snippet bytes, reference ranges and candidate SHA/file/location associations. It reads source blobs from the pinned Git objects, so local checkout edits cannot silently alter the evidence. Missing source or candidate files fail validation. `--schema-only` is an explicit weaker check. External PyTorch gates are labelled as runtime dependencies because their implementation is outside the pinned sources.

The same-engine SGLang atomic-on/off intervention has been run on the H100 stack (`sglang_qwen2.5_1.5b_gptq_marlin_stock_x6`, `_guard_off_x6`, `_guard_env_on_x6`, `_guard_off_det_x6`): one pod, one installed 0.5.19 wheel, one model revision (`4f5a0e31`), the legacy prompts, schedule and settings, and as the only change the installed `marlin_utils.py` guard, `if not True:` replaced by an `SGLANG_MARLIN_USE_ATOMIC_ADD` check with the default off ([probes/sglang_marlin_ablation/wheel_guard.patch](probes/sglang_marlin_ablation/wheel_guard.patch)). With the atomic path off, the two processes of an arm start from the same baseline hash and 4 of 12 comparisons (stock scheduler) or 6 of 12 (`--enable-deterministic-inference`) differ; with it on, on the stock wheel or on the edited wheel with the variable set to 1, 12 of 12 comparisons differ and the two processes start from different hashes. A separate one-process diagnostic on the same wheel found 0 of 512 sampled tokens changed in all 6 repeats of both path-off arms; with the stock scheduler 32 of 512 top-5 positions changed in every one of those repeats, while the deterministic-mode process was identical in all 6; with the path on, 30 tokens changed in 3 of 6 (stock) and 2 of 6 (variable set) repeats. On this stack the Marlin atomicAdd K-slice reduction is therefore the isolated cause of the recorded token-level differences and of the cross-process hash divergence. The same-length in-process differences that remain with the path off are not explained by this guard and are not attributed here; their cause is unexplained, as is the det arm's discrepancy between the census processes and the diagnostic noted below. The direct Marlin operator probes and the vLLM controls remain supporting evidence. The variable that switches the guard is not among the flags the reports record; the env-on arm's environment is attested by the pod's `/proc` environ log in [probes/sglang_marlin_ablation](probes/sglang_marlin_ablation/). A later arm on another H100 pod, with the same 204 installed distributions, the same wheel edit (stock and edited `marlin_utils.py` digests equal to the ablation's) and the same `ServerArgs` except `max_running_requests=1`, ran one request at a time (`sglang_qwen2.5_1.5b_gptq_marlin_guard_off_fixed_composition_x6`, [campaign note](probes/shape/campaigns/2026-09-15-h100-e6-matrix2.md)). Both processes gave the same observation in all 14 evaluations, where the batched guard-off arm had 4 of 12 comparisons differing and 3 distinct observations; the one-request observation equals none of those three. This fits a batching effect as the source of the remaining differences but does not isolate batch composition: running one request at a time also changes every pass's row count and limits decode graphs to batch size 1, one request per pass is the configured behaviour rather than a recorded one, it is 12 comparisons on a different pod, and that arm's SGLang kernels, the Marlin GEMM among them, were JIT-compiled on that pod at first use, while how the ablation's kernels were built is not recorded.

`runtime_evidence.relation` distinguishes `operator_example`, `end_to_end_configuration`, `control`, `not_reached` and, when independently established, `exact_kernel`. No legacy result has verified identity tying the executed binary to a pinned inventory kernel. Report paths are preserved, duplicate tags are not discarded, and stale attachments are removed on regeneration. An invalid, rejected or missing measurement cannot become `DIFFERS` or `identical`.

Several qualifications matter when interpreting the legacy records:

- PyTorch's `det` arms used `warn_only=True`. They are not strict deterministic enforcement. The `det_strict` arms reject unsupported operations and record the rejection. On the H100 stack, two fresh-process `det_strict` invocations gave bitwise-identical `index_add_`, `scatter_add_` and float `cumsum` outputs and rejected weighted `bincount` (`_bincount_cuda` has no deterministic implementation); the rejection is stored as `REJECTED` and rendered as INVALID, never as a result. Those run directories share the legacy H100 stack directory; the report pairs fresh-process comparisons only within one campaign (reports sharing a probe source digest and, where one digest ran against different libraries on a stack, recorded package versions), so the legacy `_default` rows keep their verdicts and the new campaign is listed separately.
- Generic `index_add_`, scatter and cumsum examples do not establish that an engine reached the cited call site. The bf16/fp16 GEMM sweep does not test FP8 `scaled_mm`. FlashInfer top-k does not test the distinct vLLM/SGLang implementations.
- cuBLASLt algorithm logs came from a separate diagnostic child process. They are not executed-kernel identities for the measured repeats. A stable output also does not establish that atomics were absent.
- The SM120 fused MoE (`b12x_fused_moe`, NVFP4, flashinfer 0.6.18.post1) produced different outputs on repeated calls with bit-identical inputs at 16, 512 and 4096 tokens and top-k 1, 2, 4 and 8, on RTX PRO 6000 in three campaigns: the legacy run on driver 595.91.07 (top-k 2, 4 and 8), the 14 September morning run on driver 595.71.05 (all four) and a same-day rerun on driver 595.91.07 (all four), each with 8 evaluations per configuration over two processes. No output was non-finite. Between 57% and 85% of elements differed; of those, 53% to 80% were within 2 bf16 ULPs, the remainder in an open 3-or-more bucket whose maximum ordinal distance of about 30,000 indicates elements whose sign differs between evaluations, not a relative error of that size. The differing share grows with top-k and hardly changes with the token count. In this release the static kernel (up to 640 routed rows) and the dynamic kernel scatter-add one rounded bf16x2 partial per 128-wide intermediate slice with a `red.global.add.noftz.v4.bf16x2` reduction of eight bf16 lanes (`moe_static_kernel.py:2164` and `_moe_dynamic/generic.py:2552`, helper at `moe_w4a16_fp4_helpers.py:2096-2127`, all at the pinned sha), so a top-k 1 element still receives eight reduction adds; the measurements are consistent with that design but the executed kernel was not identified and the cause was not isolated. The unquantised reference is a sanity diagnostic, not a correctness bound. The dense split-K GEMM epilogue (flashinfer-0011) is not exercised by these probes. The 595.71.05 run has in-process verdicts only, because the probe then hashed two run-dependent diagnostic values into the comparison key, a defect since fixed; the same-day rerun on driver 595.91.07 with the fixed analyser gives DIFFERS in process and across the two processes in all 12 configurations. That rerun's recorded diagnostics, bounded to it: sign changes per comparison from 8 to 15 at 16 tokens and top-k 1, rising to about 11,000 of 8,388,608 elements at 4096 tokens and top-k 8; largest absolute difference 0.0039 to 0.0215 at 16 tokens, 0.0156 to 0.0703 at 512 and 0.031 to 0.117 at 4096; largest absolute difference relative to the largest baseline magnitude 0.005 to 0.030; no non-finite values. A fourth run on the same GPU model and driver 595.91.07 used FlashInfer built from its main branch at commit 5ed46a9 (recorded as flashinfer-python 0.7.0, a development version, with nvidia-cutlass-dsl 4.7.1 instead of 4.8.0.dev0), after FlashInfer PR #4718 rewrote the static kernel to store one contribution per route and sum them in a separate fixed-order finalise and raised the NVFP4 static cutover from 640 to 1024 routed pairs. The six configurations the dispatch source sends to the static kernel (16 tokens at every top-k, 512 tokens at top-k 1 and 2) were bitwise identical in process and across two processes, 8 evaluations each; the six sent to the dynamic kernel (512 tokens at top-k 4 and 8, every 4096-token configuration), whose source is byte-identical to 0.6.18.post1, differed in both, with sign-change counts and largest absolute differences in the ranges of the same-day 0.6.18.post1 rerun. The FlashInfer source and the cutlass-dsl version both changed between those runs, the executed kernel was inferred from the dispatch source and the JIT log, and the micro kernels (at most 8 tokens) and MXFP4 were not measured ([campaign note](probes/shape/campaigns/2026-09-14-sm120-crosssku.md)). These reports share the stack directory and the probe source digest of the 0.6.18.post1 rerun, so the report tooling separates campaigns by recorded package versions as well.
- Two fixed, finite, already-rounded contributions added to zero commute; this narrow observation about finalisation does not prove the upstream GEMM deterministic. Two-rank all-reduce is similarly a weak smoke test for associativity. The three-rank cancellation case (ranks holding 256, 1 and -256 in bf16, whose rounded sum is 0 or 1 depending on the order) was run on three L40S (NCCL 2.27.7, torch 2.9.1+cu130). Each configuration was repeatable: 8 evaluations per rank, identical in process and across two processes, for the default settings and for `NCCL_ALGO=Tree NCCL_PROTO=Simple NCCL_MAX_NCHANNELS=1`, with both the cancellation and the random inputs. The two configurations did not agree with each other: the Tree result was 1.0 in every element, the default result was a mixture of the two reachable roundings, and the random-input results also differ between the settings. All rows were measured with `NCCL_P2P_DISABLE=1` because the default P2P/CUMEM transport hung on that host and was not measured; disabling P2P changes the transport and can change NCCL's algorithm choice. Reduction results should be treated as stable only for a pinned algorithm, protocol, channel count, transport and topology.
- FlashInfer plain all-reduce fusion **was run** on two H100s and returned matching hashes. NVLS and cuDNN are not established by those records. SGLang's default B200 bf16 arm has differences; the separately forced `trtllm_mha` arm has a null result. These are separate configurations.
- The `moe_wna16_cuda_*` results were obtained by calling `fused_experts` directly with an int4 W4A16 quant config on the vLLM 0.28.0 wheel. At the pinned release the kernel source is byte-identical to the measured one and is still built and exported, but no quantisation method reaches it: `MoeWNA16Method`, `AutoAWQMoEMethod` and `CompressedTensorsWNA16MoEMethod` all build their kernel through the WNA16 backend oracle, whose Triton backend is `TritonWNA16Experts`, so a direct `fused_experts` call is the only route left. They are therefore evidence about the kernel, not about stock `--quantization moe_wna16` serving on v0.27.0 or later. Reported upstream in [vllm-project/vllm#56887](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/56887).
- The four `sglang_qwen2.5_1.5b_gptq_marlin_{stock,guard_off,guard_env_on,guard_off_det}_x6` arms differ only in the installed `marlin_utils.py` guard and the `SGLANG_MARLIN_USE_ATOMIC_ADD` environment, which the reports do not record; the wheel edit, guard tests, pyc check and environment log are in [probes/sglang_marlin_ablation](probes/sglang_marlin_ablation/). The `labelled-topk-v2` reports give byte counts, not token counts; the per-repeat token counts come from a separate one-process diagnostic in the same directory, whose deterministic-mode process was identical in 6 of 6 repeats while the two census processes differed in 2 and 4 of 6, a discrepancy whose cause is not shown. The `_guard_off_fixed_composition_x6` arm's two processes ran kernels JIT-compiled by the first of them; its reports hold hashes only, so its text was not checked.

## Batch-shape experiments

[The hypothesis](docs/SHAPE_HYPOTHESIS.md) is a research question, not an established invariant. [The runbook](probes/shape/RUNBOOK.md) describes the instruments and their limits. `make shape-tables` reanalyses raw observations before printing the tables; `make check-derived` rejects stale summaries, CSV and generated README blocks.

The legacy E2 logs contain 2,001 forward passes and 115 empty scheduler calls. Empty calls retained stale batch rows in the old hook and are now excluded. At repeated recorded histories, no hidden/output-hash violation was observed. E2 also reports singleton history groups, which provide no repeatability test. The shape key now preserves batch order, target placement, parallel settings and recorded dispatch; it still does not capture every kernel planner decision.

E3 reproduced two scripted trajectories in fresh processes (37 and 42 forward passes). E3 is not a P2 test: the script injects requests after a fixed number of scheduler steps; it does not drive the second run from the first run's trace or teacher-force the recorded tokens. P2 is tested by E6. Until 15 September the E3 driver accepted `--fp8-per-tensor` and named the arm `_pertensor` without forcing the scheme; no E3 per-tensor arm exists in this repository.

Every E6 record and replay in this repository, and every verdict in the E6 table below, was produced with vLLM 0.28.0 on torch 2.13.0+cu130; they are not evidence about any other version, and in particular not about 0.29.0.

E6 is the P2 experiment. Its hook records (schema 3) carry each request's admission with its prompt token ids, every row's consumed token ids and the finished, resumed and preempted request ids; a fresh process rebuilds the schedule from that record alone, teacher-forces the recorded continuation through a vLLM logits processor, and the comparison requires equal trace length, request order, recorded shape, scheduler limits and token ids per pass before it compares hashes. E6 was first run on one stack (H100 80GB, driver 580.126.09, torch 2.13.0+cu130, vLLM 0.28.0, FlashAttention 3, compiled with FULL_AND_PIECEWISE CUDA graphs, V1 model runner, prefix caching off, greedy sampling) with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct at a pinned revision, in two arms: the plain arrival script (37 forward passes, 512 rows) and a chunked-prefill arm with a 128-token budget in which 45 of the 77 passes contain a prefill chunk, 44 of them alongside decode rows (555 rows, 43 partial prefill-chunk rows). In both arms every replayed pass met the requirements and every row's hidden-state hash, argmax and full-logit hash equalled the recorded one, so both replays are IDENTICAL; the free-running argmax under teacher forcing never departed from the recorded token in either arm. The claim is bounded as the summaries state: one recorded schedule per arm, replayed once in one fresh process on the same stack, with the compiled artefacts the record left on disk. It does not show that other schedules, models, stacks or parallel configurations replay, and the boundary rebuild described next did not reproduce the recorded bits. Neither arm survived a cold replay: with every compile and kernel cache cleared, on another machine of the same model and driver, both are DIFFERS from pass 0, slot 0, as the fourth campaign below reports.

E6 also has a boundary mode for a verifier that wants to replay one sampled pass rather than the whole recorded sequence: it prefills every row's recorded prefix in a single pass in a fresh process and then runs one decode pass with the recorded shape, graph and token ids. For pass 12 of the plain arm (16 decode rows, 1098 prefix tokens) the rebuilt decode pass matched the recorded pass in every shape field, in dispatch (a FULL graph padded to 16 tokens), in attention backend and in every consumed token id, and its argmax equalled the recorded argmax in all 16 rows, but its hidden-state and full-logit hashes differed in all 16 rows; the top-5 logprobs moved by at most 0.125 and the top-5 set changed in one row. The recorded KV state of those rows was built by two piecewise-graph prefill passes (481 and 489 scheduled tokens, padded to 496) and eleven passes with decode rows; the rebuilt one came from a single 1098-token prefill without a graph, so every cached position was produced under a different batch shape and the decode-generated positions by a different kernel phase. This one instance establishes that, on this stack, a KV state rebuilt by one prefill pass over the recorded prefix did not reproduce the recorded hidden bits of the following decode pass. It does not identify which kernel differs, does not separate the prefill-versus-decode difference from the changed prefill shape, and a single boundary at a single pass is not a rate. For a verifier the consequence is that replaying one sampled pass from its prefix is not, on this evidence, a substitute for replaying the recorded passes that produced the cache; only the full replays reproduced the shapes that built the KV state, and only they were IDENTICAL.

A second campaign the same day ran the plain arrival script on three further models, on a second H100 pod with the same recorded software stack (driver 580.126.09, torch 2.13.0+cu130, vLLM 0.28.0, FlashAttention 3, V1 model runner, TP=1, prefix caching off, greedy sampling, vLLM's compile cache disabled) and the census code unchanged ([campaign note](probes/shape/campaigns/2026-09-14-h100-e6-matrix.md)): Qwen3-8B-FP8 with its checkpoint's blockwise FP8 scheme (128 by 128 weight blocks, dynamic activation scales per token and group of 128 channels, DeepGEMM block-scaled GEMM), compiled with FULL_AND_PIECEWISE graphs; Llama 3.1 8B Instruct from the ungated NousResearch mirror, whose weight shards, configuration and tokenizer carry the same Hugging Face digests as the meta-llama repository, compiled with FULL_AND_PIECEWISE graphs; and Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B-Chat without compilation or CUDA graphs, an uncompiled configuration, which is not degenerate on this stack. Each record has 37 forward passes and 512 rows, with no preemption, and each replay met every requirement and matched the recorded hidden-state, argmax and full-logit hashes in 512 of 512 rows, so all three are IDENTICAL; the free-running argmax never departed from the forced token. None of the 48 continuations ends in a cycle of period 3 or less, and the MoE arm's first request reproduces the uncompiled E4 continuation token for token. The bounds are narrower than they look: one recorded schedule per model, each replayed once, in a new process on the same pod straight after its record and with the Inductor and DeepGEMM caches that the record had left on disk, so the replay did not recompile from scratch. A boundary rebuild for these models, compiled MoE, TP above 1 and other engines, GPUs or drivers were not run in this campaign; the uncompiled MoE arm with CUDA graphs, a chunked-prefill Qwen3-8B-FP8 arm and TP=2 were run in the next one, and all three arms were replayed cold in the fourth, where the compiled bf16 Llama arm is DIFFERS and the Qwen3-8B-FP8 and MoE arms are IDENTICAL.

A third campaign, on 15 September, used a two-GPU H100 pod (one NVLink pair, driver 580.126.09) with the same recorded versions of torch, vLLM, FlashInfer and triton; one of the 209 installed distributions, openai, differs from the earlier E6 records ([campaign note](probes/shape/campaigns/2026-09-15-h100-e6-matrix2.md)). It added six arms: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct at TP=2 with the plain and with the chunked-prefill script; Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B-Chat without compilation and with FULL CUDA graphs; Qwen3-8B-FP8 (its checkpoint's blockwise scheme) with the chunked-prefill script; and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct with online FP8, once at vLLM's default per-token dynamic activation scales and once with the harness forcing per-tensor dynamic activation scales, both compiled with FULL_AND_PIECEWISE graphs. All six replays met every requirement and matched the recorded hidden-state, argmax and full-logit hashes in every row (512 rows in the plain arms, 555 in the chunked-prefill arms), so all six are IDENTICAL. The forced per-tensor arm is the one that bears on X2, without testing it: with one activation scale per pass, taken over every row, a replay that re-supplied every neighbour's recorded tokens reproduced all 37 passes bit for bit. X2 predicts that such bits depend on the neighbours' content, and E6 re-supplies that content, so the result is consistent with X2 and is not a test of it. Per-tensor operation there is an inference, not a recorded field, because the hook observes no scale under compilation and nothing records the forcing. Four observations support it: the record and replay `env.json` files carry the digest of the corrected E6 driver script (`run_e6.py` `5fe52c55...`); every row of the first pass, whose inputs are the same in both arms, has a different hidden-state hash from the per-token arm's, as does every later row; for each of the 8 prompts that occur twice, the two prefill rows (first and second wave, different neighbours) have different hidden-state hashes, where the per-token arm, the failed attempt and the bf16 records checked have equal hashes for all 8 pairs; and the record compiled a new graph (8.82 s) where the failed attempt reused the per-token arm's (0.82 s). That first per-tensor attempt did not apply the scheme, because the E6 driver set the forcing variable after the hook had read it; its hashes equal the per-token arm's in every row, it is kept in the campaign directory rather than the results, and the driver is corrected and tested. At TP=2 vLLM listed its custom all-reduce with PyNCCL behind it as its all-reduce backends (which one served each call is not recorded), and NCCL's NVLink SHARP (NVLS) was disabled for record and replay with `NCCL_NVLS_ENABLE=0` because NCCL could not bind NVLS memory on that pod; that variable is a disclosed deviation which those arms' `env.json` files do not record (it is recorded from now on). The comparison reads rank 0's hook log, whose hashed hidden state is the input to the logit computation after the all-reduce; in these records and replays it equals rank 1's in every field but the rank number and wall time. That equality is not guaranteed: in the cold chunked-prefill TP=2 replay of the fourth campaign the two ranks' hashes differ in 4 of 555 decode rows, so at TP above 1 a verdict describes rank 0. Bounds: one H100 stack; one recorded schedule per arm, from the plain or the chunked-prefill script, replayed once in a new process on the same pod straight after its record, with the compiled-kernel caches the record left on disk (each compiled replay compiled its graph in 0.30 to 0.83 s against 3.52 to 12.01 s for its record); TP=2 on one NVLink pair with NVLS off, compared on rank 0. SGLang E6, TP above 2, the default NCCL configuration, these arms on other GPU models and any commitment to the KV cache were not run; the cold-cache replay of all eleven records is the fourth campaign below, where both TP=2 bf16 arms are DIFFERS and the FP8 and MoE arms of this campaign are IDENTICAL, and the one cross-SKU replay, described after it, is of the 14 September plain record.

A fourth campaign, later on 15 September, replayed all eleven committed E6 records once more on a different two-GPU H100 pod running the same vLLM 0.28.0 stack, with every compile and kernel cache emptied and verified empty before each replay and with the same driver, the same 209 installed distributions bar openai in the five 14 September records, and weight files matching the Hugging Face API in all 46 ([campaign note](probes/shape/campaigns/2026-09-15-h100-e6-cold.md)). **Six of the eleven replayed bit for bit and five did not, and the five are every compiled bf16 arm.** The four compiled FP8 arms and the two uncompiled MoE arms are IDENTICAL in every row. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct in bf16 at TP=1 in both arms, at TP=2 in both arms, and Llama 3.1 8B Instruct are DIFFERS, with the hidden-state and full-logit hashes differing from pass 0, slot 0, in 493 to 555 rows per arm; every comparison requirement held, teacher forcing kept every token, and the free-running argmax moved in at most one row per arm, each time at a position where the top two logprobs are tied on one side and one bf16 step of 0.125 apart on the other. Cold-cache proof: each replay started from zero files in every cache location, wrote 165 to 1,027 cache files, rebuilt FlashInfer's sampling JIT and, for the FP8 checkpoint, all 1,735 DeepGEMM kernels at 11.61 kernels per second, the rate that arm's record reports and not the 3,073.54 of its warm same-pod replay, and no log contains a cached-artefact load. The machine claim is not the same for every arm: the two Qwen2.5-7B TP=1 arms were recorded in another datacentre, so a different machine is certain; the Llama, Qwen3-8B-FP8 plain and MoE arms of the second campaign were recorded at a different address in the same location, so a different host is likely and unproven; and the six arms of the third campaign, which include both TP=2 arms, were recorded at the same public address as the replay pod on different GPUs, so for those only different GPUs are established. Three diagnostics bound the cause. Running the plain and chunked-prefill replays with the record's own environment, so that the whole resolved configuration including the compile-cache key is byte-identical to the record's, gives the same verdict and the same differing-row counts, so vLLM's disabled compile cache is not the cause. A second cold replay of the plain record differs from the first in 5 of 512 rows, all decode rows, while agreeing with the third in every row, so a single IDENTICAL verdict in these campaigns is an observation about the runs made rather than a property of the configuration. The two cold compiles whose caches were kept chose identical Inductor tuning configurations for all ten kernels, three of them fused add-and-RMS-norm reductions whose block size is chosen by benchmarking. Why the split falls where it does is not established: a benchmark-chosen reduction configuration differing between the record's compile and a fresh one fits, and so does FP8 activation quantisation absorbing a small upstream difference that a bf16 path carries, and no record separates them. Also unexplained is one cross-rank result: in the TP=2 chunked-prefill cold replay, rank 1's hidden-state hash differs from rank 0's in 4 of 555 decode rows with the full-logit hash and the argmax equal, where the record and the warm replay have none, so at TP above 1 an IDENTICAL verdict is a statement about rank 0.

The plain H100 record has also been replayed once on an RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition (driver 595.91.07) with the same 209 installed distributions (torch 2.13.0+cu130, vLLM 0.28.0, flashinfer-python 0.6.16.post3, triton 3.7.1), the same compilation and graph modes, runner and scheduler limits, a fresh compile, and a record byte-identical to the one in this repository ([campaign note](probes/shape/campaigns/2026-09-14-sm120-crosssku.md)). ## Verification implications

A recorded batch history can help reproduce an execution, but a history chosen by the operator can still carry operator-chosen information. Logging or committing that choice does not remove the choice. [Findings notes](docs/FINDINGS.md) distinguish replay soundness from scheduler freedom and give the correct conditional bound on distinguishable trajectories. No covert-channel capacity or complete security guarantee was measured here; comparison with tolerance-based verification such as [DiFR](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20621v1) needs a specified threat model.
