# fp-atomics-census

A source census of floating-point atomic operations in LLM inference engines, with runtime observations on selected GPU stacks. It identifies potential obstacles to bit-exact replay; it does not certify a deployment or establish a complete verification protocol.

Eight repositories are pinned to 6 July 2026 commits in [scan-manifest.json](scan-manifest.json): vLLM, SGLang, FlashInfer, FlashAttention, Marlin, DeepGEMM, DeepEP and selected CUTLASS reduction paths. The [inventory](inventory/) contains source classifications and supporting references. Scanner matches and unresolved triage are recorded separately. The GPU measurements were made on 6, 7 and 14 September 2026 against installed wheels and, for one FlashInfer run, a source build of its main branch; their versions differ from the source census. The 14 September campaigns are described in [probes/shape/campaigns](probes/shape/campaigns/).

## What this adds

[Cankaya (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00279v1) already distinguishes genuine run-to-run nondeterminism from changes caused by batch size, and lists per-pass batch information for replay (§§3.2 and 6). [Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/) also treats batch invariance explicitly. This repository extends the source inventory and tests selected configurations. It does not present batch dependence as a new discovery or refute those works with experiments outside their stated scope.

The source reading identifies order-dependent accumulation in some adapter, quantisation, MoE, sampling and model-specific paths. Other paths use serialisation, one writer per destination, exact integer operations or opaque libraries. No order-dependent atomic was identified in the reviewed ordinary dense vLLM forward source paths under the stated configurations. That is a bounded source observation: generated kernels, library internals, unresolved candidates and other sources of nondeterminism prevent a universal conclusion. Throughput costs and protocol soundness were not measured here.

Unresolved candidates may be duplicate matches, helper definitions or additional sites. Their number is not a count of missed floating-point atomics. The ledger makes the unfinished triage explicit; source coverage is not claimed to be exhaustive.

Classes are **A** (potentially order-dependent floating-point accumulation), **A1** (one writer per destination), **A2** (serialised order), **A3** (gated path), **B** (exact or order-independent operation under the row's assumptions), **B-indirect** (an integer atomic can determine an order consumed by floating-point code), and **C** (opaque library or binary). Read `path`, `default_path_condition`, `gate`, `confidence` and `evidence` together. A default path is reachable on the named profile, not on every deployment. A source classification is not a measured runtime verdict.

`make validate` checks the schema, pinned checkout HEADs, exact ordered snippet bytes, reference ranges and candidate SHA/file/location associations. It reads source blobs from the pinned Git objects, so local checkout edits cannot silently alter the evidence. Missing source or candidate files fail validation. `--schema-only` is an explicit weaker check. External PyTorch gates are labelled as runtime dependencies because their implementation is outside the pinned sources.

With the atomic path off, the two processes of an arm start from the same baseline hash and 4 of 12 comparisons (stock scheduler) or 6 of 12 (`--enable-deterministic-inference`) differ; with it on, on the stock wheel or on the edited wheel with the variable set to 1, 12 of 12 comparisons differ and the two processes start from different hashes. A separate one-process diagnostic on the same wheel found 0 of 512 sampled tokens changed in all 6 repeats of both path-off arms; with the stock scheduler 32 of 512 top-5 positions changed in every one of those repeats, while the deterministic-mode process was identical in all 6; with the path on, 30 tokens changed in 3 of 6 (stock) and 2 of 6 (variable set) repeats. The same-length in-process differences that remain with the path off are not explained by this guard and are not attributed here; their cause is unexplained, as is the det arm's discrepancy between the census processes and the diagnostic noted below. The direct Marlin operator probes and the vLLM controls remain supporting evidence. The variable that switches the guard is not among the flags the reports record; the env-on arm's environment is attested by the pod's `/proc` environ log in [probes/sglang_marlin_ablation](probes/sglang_marlin_ablation/).

`runtime_evidence.relation` distinguishes `operator_example`, `end_to_end_configuration`, `control`, `not_reached` and, when independently established, `exact_kernel`. No legacy result has verified identity tying the executed binary to a pinned inventory kernel. Report paths are preserved, duplicate tags are not discarded, and stale attachments are removed on regeneration. An invalid, rejected or missing measurement cannot become `DIFFERS` or `identical`.

Several qualifications matter when interpreting the legacy records:

- PyTorch's `det` arms used `warn_only=True`. They are not strict deterministic enforcement. The `det_strict` arms reject unsupported operations and record the rejection. On the H100 stack, two fresh-process `det_strict` invocations gave bitwise-identical `index_add_`, `scatter_add_` and float `cumsum` outputs and rejected weighted `bincount` (`_bincount_cuda` has no deterministic implementation); the rejection is stored as `REJECTED` and rendered as INVALID, never as a result. Those run directories share the legacy H100 stack directory; the report pairs fresh-process comparisons only within one campaign (reports sharing a probe source digest and, where one digest ran against different libraries on a stack, recorded package versions), so the legacy `_default` rows keep their verdicts and the new campaign is listed separately.
- Generic `index_add_`, scatter and cumsum examples do not establish that an engine reached the cited call site. The bf16/fp16 GEMM sweep does not test FP8 `scaled_mm`. FlashInfer top-k does not test the distinct vLLM/SGLang implementations.
- cuBLASLt algorithm logs came from a separate diagnostic child process. They are not executed-kernel identities for the measured repeats. A stable output also does not establish that atomics were absent.
- The SM120 fused MoE (`b12x_fused_moe`, NVFP4, flashinfer 0.6.18.post1) produced different outputs on repeated calls with bit-identical inputs at 16, 512 and 4096 tokens and top-k 1, 2, 4 and 8, on RTX PRO 6000 in three campaigns: the legacy run on driver 595.91.07 (top-k 2, 4 and 8), the 14 September morning run on driver 595.71.05 (all four) and a same-day rerun on driver 595.91.07 (all four), each with 8 evaluations per configuration over two processes. No output was non-finite. Between 57% and 85% of elements differed; of those, 53% to 80% were within 2 bf16 ULPs, the remainder in an open 3-or-more bucket whose maximum ordinal distance of about 30,000 indicates elements whose sign differs between evaluations, not a relative error of that size. The differing share grows with top-k and hardly changes with the token count. In this release the static kernel (up to 640 routed rows) and the dynamic kernel scatter-add one rounded bf16x2 partial per 128-wide intermediate slice with a relaxed bf16x2 `red` reduction (`red.global.add.noftz.v4.bf16x2`), so a top-k 1 element still receives eight reduction adds; the measurements are consistent with that design but the executed kernel was not identified and the cause was not isolated. The unquantised reference is a sanity diagnostic, not a correctness bound. The dense split-K GEMM epilogue (flashinfer-0011) is not exercised by these probes. The 595.71.05 run has in-process verdicts only, because the probe then hashed two run-dependent diagnostic values into the comparison key, a defect since fixed; the same-day rerun on driver 595.91.07 with the fixed analyser gives DIFFERS in process and across the two processes in all 12 configurations. That rerun's recorded diagnostics, bounded to it: sign changes per comparison from 8 to 15 at 16 tokens and top-k 1, rising to about 11,000 of 8,388,608 elements at 4096 tokens and top-k 8; largest absolute difference 0.0039 to 0.0215 at 16 tokens, 0.0156 to 0.0703 at 512 and 0.031 to 0.117 at 4096; largest absolute difference relative to the largest baseline magnitude 0.005 to 0.030; no non-finite values. A fourth run on the same GPU model and driver 595.91.07 used FlashInfer built from its main branch at commit 5ed46a9 (recorded as flashinfer-python 0.7.0, a development version, with nvidia-cutlass-dsl 4.7.1 instead of 4.8.0.dev0), after FlashInfer PR #4718 rewrote the static kernel to store one contribution per route and sum them in a separate fixed-order finalise and raised the NVFP4 static cutover from 640 to 1024 routed pairs. The six configurations the dispatch source sends to the static kernel (16 tokens at every top-k, 512 tokens at top-k 1 and 2) were bitwise identical in process and across two processes, 8 evaluations each; the six sent to the dynamic kernel (512 tokens at top-k 4 and 8, every 4096-token configuration), whose source is byte-identical to 0.6.18.post1, differed in both, with sign-change counts and largest absolute differences in the ranges of the same-day 0.6.18.post1 rerun. The FlashInfer source and the cutlass-dsl version both changed between those runs, the executed kernel was inferred from the dispatch source and the JIT log, and the micro kernels (at most 8 tokens) and MXFP4 were not measured ([campaign note](probes/shape/campaigns/2026-09-14-sm120-crosssku.md)). These reports share the stack directory and the probe source digest of the 0.6.18.post1 rerun, so the report tooling separates campaigns by recorded package versions as well.
- Two fixed, finite, already-rounded contributions added to zero commute; this narrow observation about finalisation does not prove the upstream GEMM deterministic. Two-rank all-reduce is similarly a weak smoke test for associativity. The three-rank cancellation case (ranks holding 256, 1 and -256 in bf16, whose rounded sum is 0 or 1 depending on the order) was run on three L40S (NCCL 2.27.7, torch 2.9.1+cu130). Each configuration was repeatable: 8 evaluations per rank, identical in process and across two processes, for the default settings and for `NCCL_ALGO=Tree NCCL_PROTO=Simple NCCL_MAX_NCHANNELS=1`, with both the cancellation and the random inputs. The two configurations did not agree with each other: the Tree result was 1.0 in every element, the default result was a mixture of the two reachable roundings, and the random-input results also differ between the settings. All rows were measured with `NCCL_P2P_DISABLE=1` because the default P2P/CUMEM transport hung on that host and was not measured; disabling P2P changes the transport and can change NCCL's algorithm choice. Reduction results should be treated as stable only for a pinned algorithm, protocol, channel count, transport and topology.
- FlashInfer plain all-reduce fusion **was run** on two H100s and returned matching hashes. NVLS and cuDNN are not established by those records. SGLang's default B200 bf16 arm has differences; the separately forced `trtllm_mha` arm has a null result. These are separate configurations.
- The `moe_wna16_cuda_*` results were obtained by calling `fused_experts` directly with an int4 W4A16 quant config on the vLLM 0.28.0 wheel. On that release the engine's `moe_wna16` quantisation method no longer reaches the CUDA kernel (from v0.27.0 it uses `TritonWNA16Experts`), so they are evidence about the kernel, not about stock `--quantization moe_wna16` serving on 0.27.0 or later. Reported upstream in [vllm-project/vllm#56887](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/56887).
- The four `sglang_qwen2.5_1.5b_gptq_marlin_{stock,guard_off,guard_env_on,guard_off_det}_x6` arms differ only in the installed `marlin_utils.py` guard and the `SGLANG_MARLIN_USE_ATOMIC_ADD` environment, which the reports do not record; the wheel edit, guard tests, pyc check and environment log are in [probes/sglang_marlin_ablation](probes/sglang_marlin_ablation/). The `labelled-topk-v2` reports give byte counts, not token counts; the per-repeat token counts come from a separate one-process diagnostic in the same directory, whose deterministic-mode process was identical in 6 of 6 repeats while the two census processes differed in 2 and 4 of 6, a discrepancy whose cause is not shown.

## Batch-shape experiments

[The hypothesis](docs/SHAPE_HYPOTHESIS.md) is a research question, not an established invariant. [The runbook](probes/shape/RUNBOOK.md) describes the instruments and their limits. `make shape-tables` reanalyses raw observations before printing the tables; `make check-derived` rejects stale summaries, CSV and generated README blocks.

The legacy E2 logs contain 2,001 forward passes and 115 empty scheduler calls. Empty calls retained stale batch rows in the old hook and are now excluded. At repeated recorded histories, no hidden/output-hash violation was observed. E2 also reports singleton history groups, which provide no repeatability test. The shape key now preserves batch order, target placement, parallel settings and recorded dispatch; it still does not capture every kernel planner decision.

E3 reproduced two scripted trajectories in fresh processes (37 and 42 forward passes). E3 is not a P2 test: the script injects requests after a fixed number of scheduler steps; it does not drive the second run from the first run's trace or teacher-force the recorded tokens. P2 is tested by E6.

E6 is the P2 experiment. Its hook records (schema 3) carry each request's admission with its prompt token ids, every row's consumed token ids and the finished, resumed and preempted request ids; a fresh process rebuilds the schedule from that record alone, teacher-forces the recorded continuation through a vLLM logits processor, and the comparison requires equal trace length, request order, recorded shape, scheduler limits and token ids per pass before it compares hashes. E6 was first run on one stack (H100 80GB, driver 580.126.09, torch 2.13.0+cu130, vLLM 0.28.0, FlashAttention 3, compiled with FULL_AND_PIECEWISE CUDA graphs, V1 model runner, prefix caching off, greedy sampling) with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct at a pinned revision, in two arms: the plain arrival script (37 forward passes, 512 rows) and a chunked-prefill arm with a 128-token budget in which 45 of the 77 passes contain a prefill chunk, 44 of them alongside decode rows (555 rows, 43 partial prefill-chunk rows). In both arms every replayed pass met the requirements and every row's hidden-state hash, argmax and full-logit hash equalled the recorded one, so both replays are IDENTICAL; the free-running argmax under teacher forcing never departed from the recorded token in either arm. The claim is bounded as the summaries state: one recorded schedule per arm, replayed once in one fresh process on the same stack. It does not show that other schedules, models, stacks or parallel configurations replay, and the boundary rebuild described next did not reproduce the recorded bits.

E6 also has a boundary mode for a verifier that wants to replay one sampled pass rather than the whole recorded sequence: it prefills every row's recorded prefix in a single pass in a fresh process and then runs one decode pass with the recorded shape, graph and token ids. For pass 12 of the plain arm (16 decode rows, 1098 prefix tokens) the rebuilt decode pass matched the recorded pass in every shape field, in dispatch (a FULL graph padded to 16 tokens), in attention backend and in every consumed token id, and its argmax equalled the recorded argmax in all 16 rows, but its hidden-state and full-logit hashes differed in all 16 rows; the top-5 logprobs moved by at most 0.125 and the top-5 set changed in one row. The recorded KV state of those rows was built by two piecewise-graph prefill passes (481 and 489 scheduled tokens, padded to 496) and eleven passes with decode rows; the rebuilt one came from a single 1098-token prefill without a graph, so every cached position was produced under a different batch shape and the decode-generated positions by a different kernel phase. This one instance establishes that, on this stack, a KV state rebuilt by one prefill pass over the recorded prefix did not reproduce the recorded hidden bits of the following decode pass. It does not identify which kernel differs, does not separate the prefill-versus-decode difference from the changed prefill shape, and a single boundary at a single pass is not a rate. For a verifier the consequence is that replaying one sampled pass from its prefix is not, on this evidence, a substitute for replaying the recorded passes that produced the cache; only the full replays reproduced the shapes that built the KV state, and only they were IDENTICAL.

A second campaign the same day ran the plain arrival script on three further models, on a second H100 pod with the same recorded software stack (driver 580.126.09, torch 2.13.0+cu130, vLLM 0.28.0, FlashAttention 3, V1 model runner, TP=1, prefix caching off, greedy sampling, vLLM's compile cache disabled) and the census code unchanged ([campaign note](probes/shape/campaigns/2026-09-14-h100-e6-matrix.md)): Qwen3-8B-FP8 with its checkpoint's blockwise FP8 scheme (128 by 128 weight blocks, dynamic activation scales per token and group of 128 channels, DeepGEMM block-scaled GEMM), compiled with FULL_AND_PIECEWISE graphs; Llama 3.1 8B Instruct from the ungated NousResearch mirror, whose weight shards, configuration and tokenizer carry the same Hugging Face digests as the meta-llama repository, compiled with FULL_AND_PIECEWISE graphs; and Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B-Chat without compilation or CUDA graphs, the configuration that is not degenerate on this stack. Each record has 37 forward passes and 512 rows, with no preemption, and each replay met every requirement and matched the recorded hidden-state, argmax and full-logit hashes in 512 of 512 rows, so all three are IDENTICAL; the free-running argmax never departed from the forced token. None of the 48 continuations ends in a cycle of period 3 or less, and the MoE arm's first request reproduces the uncompiled E4 continuation token for token. The bounds are narrower than they look: one recorded schedule per model, each replayed once, in a new process on the same pod straight after its record and with the Inductor and DeepGEMM caches that the record had left on disk, so the replay did not recompile from scratch. The uncompiled MoE arm with CUDA graphs, a chunked-prefill Qwen3-8B-FP8 arm, a boundary rebuild for these models, compiled MoE, TP above 1 and other engines, GPUs or drivers were not run in this campaign.

Forced per-tensor FP8 differed from step 0 in all four arms. In every compiled MoE record here, legacy and new, the target's generated tokens settle into a short repeating cycle, whereas the uncompiled arms generate the same non-repeating continuation in both campaigns, token for token. The compiled MoE arms therefore ran a mis-compiled model: torch.compile, not CUDA graphs, not the hook and not the compile cache, changes this model's output on this stack. Their DIFFERS verdicts are observations on a degenerate configuration and are not evidence for prediction P4; the rows stay in the table. Reported upstream in [vllm-project/vllm#56900](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/56900). The uncompiled MoE arms, whose output is not degenerate, were IDENTICAL under neighbour replacement even though per-expert counts changed, so on this stack the MoE content coupling predicted by X1 was not observed in a valid configuration. This is one model on one stack. The hook observed the per-tensor scale only on uncompiled passes that were not graph replays: under compilation vLLM ran the quantisation through its native path (`custom_ops=['none']` in the recorded configuration), which the hook does not wrap, and graph replay bypasses Python wrappers. Changed counts and scales remain mechanism diagnostics, not causal isolation. These are six-repeat, one-slot observations on short prompts and one stack.

## Verification implications

A recorded batch history can help reproduce an execution, but a history chosen by the operator can still carry operator-chosen information. Logging or committing that choice does not remove the choice. [Findings notes](docs/FINDINGS.md) distinguish replay soundness from scheduler freedom and give the correct conditional bound on distinguishable trajectories. No covert-channel capacity or complete security guarantee was measured here; comparison with tolerance-based verification such as [DiFR](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20621v1) needs a specified threat model.

GPU requirements are exploratory; use separate environments, supply explicit model revisions and archive the complete installed environment for each new campaign. Missing weight, tokenizer, cubin, topology or selected-kernel hashes are explicitly unknown; a package version alone does not provide them.
